About Us

Who We Are

We're young people in northern Tanzania who care about the land, the wildlife, and the communities that depend on both. We watch, we learn, and we act.

Our Mission, Vision, and Motto

Our Mission

To watch what's changing in the wild around us, understand why, and act before it's too late — through conservation, education, and working together.

Our Vision

To see wildlife thrive and communities prosper together — where the next generation knows its own land and stands ready to protect it.

Our Motto

Protect Nature — Protect Life

Our Purpose

WildGuard Society started with a simple idea: the people who live closest to wildlife often know it best, yet their voice is the last one heard.

Rivers are drying up. Grasslands are shrinking. Animals that used to pass through our villages are disappearing. Instead of waiting for someone else to fix it, we bring young people together to understand what's changing, document it with our phones and cameras, and do something about it — through clean-ups, tree planting, education, and working side by side with farmers, herders, and village rangers.

What We Do

Our core pillars of action

Observation

We keep an eye on the bush around us — noting which species are showing up, which ones are vanishing, and what's changing in the land.

Adaptation

We adjust with the land — finding practical, nature-friendly ways to cope with drought, shrinking pasture, and other pressures on our communities.

Action

We roll up our sleeves — tree planting, clean-ups, raising awareness, and standing up for the places and animals we care about.

Collaboration

We bring young people together and work hand in hand with farmers, herders, and village rangers — because the bush is home to all of us.

Why Youth?

Young people aren't just the future — we're living through these changes right now. Water that used to be close is now a long walk away. Grazing land is thinner. Wild animals are harder to see. When you grow up close to the land, you notice these things early, and you feel them deeply. That's why we act now, not later — and why we're building a generation that knows its own home well enough to protect it.
