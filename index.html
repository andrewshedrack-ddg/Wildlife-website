Tanzania's Magnificent Wildlife

Home to some of Africa's most incredible species

African Lion

Habitat: Savannah grasslands

Status: Vulnerable

The only cats that live in groups called prides, with complex social structures and cooperative hunting.

African Elephant

Habitat: Forests & savannahs

Status: Endangered

Earth's largest land mammal, known for incredible memory, strong family bonds, and vital role as ecosystem engineers.

Leopard

Habitat: Various habitats

Status: Vulnerable

The most elusive of the big cats, exceptional climbers that can carry prey twice their weight up into trees.

African Buffalo

Habitat: Floodplains & savannahs

Status: Near Threatened

Known for their unpredictable nature and considered one of Africa's most dangerous animals, yet vital to ecosystem balance.

Black Rhinoceros

Habitat: Grasslands & savannahs

Status: Critically Endangered

Distinguished by their hooked upper lip, black rhinos are critically endangered due to poaching for their horns.

Cheetah

Habitat: Open grasslands

Status: Vulnerable

The fastest land animal, capable of reaching speeds up to 70 mph in short bursts covering distances up to 500m.

Tanzania's Protected Sanctuaries

World-renowned national parks and conservation areas

Serengeti National Park

Size: 14,763 km²

Established: 1951

Home to the Great Migration of over 1.5 million wildebeest, 200,000 zebras, and countless predators following the rains.

Ngorongoro Conservation Area

Size: 8,292 km²

Established: 1959

Features the world's largest inactive volcanic caldera, creating a natural enclosure for incredible wildlife density including the Big Five.

Tarangire National Park

Size: 2,850 km²

Established: 1970

Famous for its high concentration of elephants and ancient baobab trees, especially during the dry season when wildlife gathers along the Tarangire River.

Selous Game Reserve

Size: 50,000 km²

Established: 1922

One of the largest faunal reserves in the world, featuring diverse habitats from miombo woodlands to open grasslands and supporting incredible biodiversity.

Our Conservation Work

Protecting wildlife through technology and community action

🔍

AI-Powered Monitoring

Using camera traps and acoustic sensors to track wildlife movements, detect poaching threats, and gather vital data for conservation planning.

🤝

Community Partnerships

Working with local communities to create sustainable livelihoods that reduce human-wildlife conflict while providing economic benefits.

🛡️

Anti-Poaching Patrols

Deploying trained ranger teams equipped with modern technology to patrol protected areas and respond rapidly to threats.

🌱

Habitat Restoration

Rehabilitating degraded ecosystems through reforestation, water source protection, and invasive species management to support wildlife populations.
