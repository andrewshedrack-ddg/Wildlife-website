Tanzania's Magnificent Wildlife

Home to some of Africa's most incredible species

African Lion

Scientific Name: Panthera leo

Conservation Status: Vulnerable

Weight Range: 330 - 550 lbs (150 - 250 kg)

Key Detail: A male's mane isn't just for show; a darker, thicker mane indicates top health and higher testosterone, which helps them attract mates and intimidate rivals.

African Bush Elephant

Scientific Name: Loxodonta africana

Conservation Status: Endangered

Weight Range: 4 - 7 tons (3,600 - 6,300 kg)

Key Detail: Their massive ears act like built-in air conditioners. By flapping them, elephants can cool down the blood flowing through the large vessels in their ears to drop their body temperature.

Cape Buffalo

Scientific Name: Syncerus caffer

Conservation Status: Near Threatened

Weight Range: 1,100 - 2,200 lbs (500 - 1,000 kg)

Key Detail: An adult male’s horns fuse together at the base across the forehead, forming a solid bone shield known as a 'boss' that can even stop bullets.

African Leopard

Scientific Name: Panthera pardus pardus

Conservation Status: Vulnerable

Weight Range: 65 - 200 lbs (30 - 90 kg)

Key Detail: To protect their food from scavengers like lions and hyenas, leopards can haul prey weighing as much as themselves entirely up into the branches of trees.

Black Rhinoceros

Scientific Name: Diceros bicornis

Conservation Status: Critically Endangered

Weight Range: 1,800 - 3,100 lbs (800 - 1,400 kg)

Key Detail: They have a hooked, prehensile upper lip used like a thumb to strip leaves and twigs from branches, distinct from the square-lipped White rhino.

Tanzania's National Parks

Explore Tanzania's protected wilderness areas

Serengeti National Park

Location: Northern Tanzania

Primary Feature: The Great Wildebeest Migration

Highlight: A UNESCO World Heritage Site famous for hosting the largest overland wildlife migration on earth, where over 1.5 million wildebeest and zebra traverse the endless plains.

Ngorongoro Conservation Area

Location: Northern Tanzania

Primary Feature: The Ngorongoro Crater

Highlight: The world's largest intact, unfilled volcanic caldera. It forms a pristine natural enclosure that shelters over 25,000 large mammals, including a dense population of predators and rare black rhinos.

Tarangire National Park

Location: Manyara Region

Primary Feature: Baobab Trees & Massive Elephant Herds

Highlight: Famous for its iconic landscape dotted with ancient, giant Baobab trees and its high concentration of elephants, which gather around the Tarangire River during the dry season.

Lake Manyara National Park

Location: Arusha & Manyara Regions

Primary Feature: Tree-Climbing Lions & Soda Lake

Highlight: A diverse park featuring an alkaline lake filled with thousands of pink flamingos, dense ground-water forests, and its legendary residents: rare tree-climbing lions.

Ruaha National Park

Location: Central/Southern Tanzania

Primary Feature: Rugged Wilderness & Large Lion Prides

Highlight: Tanzania's hidden gem, offering a remote and untouched safari landscape. It is famous for its dramatic Great Ruaha River scenery, huge herds of buffalo, and exceptionally large lion prides.
