Our Animals

The Big Five & Beyond

Discover Tanzania's most iconic wildlife species, from the mighty lion to the elusive leopard.

Big Five

African Lion

📍 Savannah | 🔰 Vulnerable

The king of the savannah, lions are the only cats that live in prides, with complex social structures and powerful hunting skills.

Big Five

African Elephant

📍 Forest & Savannah | 🔰 Endangered

Earth's largest land mammal, elephants are known for their incredible memory, complex emotions, and vital role as ecosystem engineers.

Big Five

Leopard

📍 Savannah & Forest | 🔰 Vulnerable

The most elusive of the Big Five, leopards are master climbers that can haul prey twice their weight high into trees.

Big Five

African Buffalo

📍 Floodplains & Savannah | 🔰 Near Threatened

Known for their unpredictable nature and considered one of the most dangerous animals in Africa, African buffaloes are formidable defenders of their herd.

Big Five

Black Rhinoceros

📍 Grasslands & Savannah | 🔰 Critically Endangered

Black rhinos have a hooked upper lip, unlike the square lip of white rhinos, and are critically endangered due to poaching for their horns.

Legendary National Parks

Explore Tanzania's world-renowned conservation areas, home to the greatest wildlife spectacle on Earth.

Serengeti National Park

14,763 km² | Established 1951

Witness the Great Migration of 1.5 million wildebeest and 200,000 zebras. A UNESCO World Heritage Site and icon of African wildlife.

Ngorongoro Conservation Area

8,292 km² | Established 1959

The world's largest inactive volcanic caldera, hosting the densest population of lions in Africa along with the critically endangered black rhino.

Tarangire National Park

2,850 km² | Established 1970

Famous for its ancient baobab trees and massive elephant herds that converge along the Tarangire River during the dry season.

Selous Game Reserve

50,000 km² | Established 1922

One of the largest faunal reserves in the world, featuring diverse habitats from miombo woodlands to vast wetlands and grasslands.
